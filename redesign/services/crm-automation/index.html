The work this solves.

Contacts move between forms, inboxes, calendars, and spreadsheets. Follow-up depends on memory, booked contacts remain in outreach lists, and stage changes do not always create the right task.

What the project can include.

AiXCEL connects the business workflow to the implementation. We agree on the first useful outcome before expanding the scope.

  • A defined contact and opportunity lifecycle
  • Form, calendar, and CRM field mapping
  • Owner routing, duplicate checks, and task creation rules
  • Booking and opt-out checks before any follow-up
  • An exception view and documented operating procedure

The checks matter.

Acceptance is based on the agreed workflow. Useful tests include:

  • Confirm a booked contact is excluded from the next outreach step.
  • Verify assignment for a new, returning, and duplicate contact.
  • Check who can read and change the records before rollout.

A sensible first scope.

Begin with one intake-to-handoff path. Expand after the team accepts the behavior.

Who this fits.

Agencies and service businesses whose CRM no longer reflects how the team works.

What happens before you quote?

We review the workflow, its owners, the tools involved, and the access needed. The proposal defines the scope, assumptions, responsibilities, and acceptance criteria.

Do we have to replace our current tools?

Often the first improvement can use existing platforms and APIs. Tool changes are considered when the current setup cannot meet the agreed requirements.

What happens after handover?

Documentation and an operating checklist are part of the agreed delivery. Ongoing monitoring, improvements, and support are scoped separately.

Can the AI send messages or change records on its own?

External actions need explicit authorization and tested rules. Drafting, review, and execution are separate steps when the decision has consequences.
