Our approach

Start small. Make it dependable.

A practical path from the current workflow to a scoped implementation, tested behavior, and a usable handover.

Example workflowHuman review

1. Map the actual process.

Walk through a recent example of the work. Identify the trigger, source records, tools, owners, decisions, and points where people wait or repeat themselves.

2. Agree on a useful first result.

Choose a bounded workflow and write down what is included, what depends on access or data quality, and who accepts the result. The proposal sets scope and responsibilities before implementation.

3. Build and test the difficult paths.

Connect the minimum useful set of systems. Test the ordinary path as well as missing information, duplicates, unavailable providers, and approval changes. Keep evidence with the behavior being tested.

4. Hand over something the team can operate.

Document the setup, train the responsible people, and show how to inspect a run, resolve an exception, or pause the system. Customer access and credentials stay in the agreed customer-controlled environment.

5. Improve from observed use.

Once the team has accepted the first workflow, evaluate what it changes in practice. Ongoing monitoring, maintenance, and additional scope are agreed separately.

Is discovery always followed by a build?

No. The useful outcome may be a simpler process, a change to an existing tool, or a decision to postpone implementation.

How do you define success?

With the owner before building: a clear input, expected behavior, difficult cases, and a practical acceptance check.

Can you work with our existing tools?

Yes, subject to their current capabilities, access, and integration constraints. These are inspected during discovery.
